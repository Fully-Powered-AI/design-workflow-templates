## The outcome

A redesign starts from a written record of what the site is today, not from memory. The design lead holds one inventory of the pages that were captured, what each says and shows, which patterns recur across them and where those patterns differ. Pages that could not be read, such as ones behind a sign-in, ones that change for each visitor or ones nobody captured, are named as outside the evidence so no one assumes they were covered.

The lead confirms the baseline, corrects it and decides the scope of the redesign. The workflow template records; it does not judge.

## Who starts the work, and when

The design lead starts it when a redesign is being scoped and the current site needs to be written down first. People who capture pages, such as a designer or a content owner, prepare the inputs. The same baseline can be rerun on a later set of captures to see what has changed since the first.

## What they supply

- **A page list** as a table: one row per page, with its address, name, section, the capture file, the capture date, how it was captured and an access note.
- **The captures:** screenshots, saved page text or saved page source, taken by a person with their own access. Nothing is fetched for them.
- **Settings:** the site's name, why the redesign is happening in one sentence, the page limit, and any names the team already uses for its patterns.

The workflow template opens no website, takes no capture and never asks for a password, a login code or a key.

## What comes out

One baseline in a fixed order: what was supplied, coverage by section, a record for each page read, the patterns that recur and where they vary, the elements seen once, the places where pages say different things, the pages outside the evidence with the reason for each, names visible in the navigation that have no row in the page list, questions for the lead, and a confirmation block for the lead to fill in.

Where a section has nothing in it, it says so in words rather than being left out.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your page list columns.** Use the default headers or rename your export's columns once. When a header does not match, the assistant stops and asks.
- **Your sections.** Group pages the way your team talks about the site.
- **Your page limit.** Choose how many pages one baseline covers.
- **Your pattern names.** Supply the names your team already uses, or accept descriptive ones.
- **Your access words.** Say which words in the access note mean a page is restricted, personalized or not visited.
- **Who confirms.** Name the design lead and who else reads the baseline before the scope is set.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
